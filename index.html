02 / chronology

The story so far

WBD was created by combining WarnerMedia and Discovery in April 2022. The combination brought together premium film and television production, HBO, cable networks, news and sports, but also left the group managing a substantial debt load and two very different operating environments: growth-oriented streaming and studios, and mature linear television networks. The June 2025 separation plan was intended to give those businesses distinct capital-market identities.

APRIL 2022

Warner Bros. Discovery is formed

WarnerMedia and Discovery complete their combination. The new group owns a broad collection of studios, streaming services, cable networks, sports and news assets. The scale creates breadth, but the company also has to manage integration, debt and changing television economics.

JUNE 2025

WBD announces a planned separation

WBD outlines a split between Streaming & Studios and Global Networks. The latter would include linear news, sports and entertainment channels. This separation becomes the structural foundation of the Netflix proposal.

4–5 DEC 2025

Netflix agreement is announced

Netflix agrees to acquire the film and television studios, HBO and HBO Max after the separation of Global Networks. The announced value is $27.75 per WBD share, with $23.25 cash and $4.50 in Netflix shares at announcement; enterprise value is approximately $82.7bn. Netflix announcement.

8 DEC 2025

Paramount launches a $30 all-cash tender offer

Paramount seeks all of WBD, including Global Networks. Its tender offer is made directly to shareholders rather than relying only on a negotiated board recommendation. Paramount’s filed presentation describes $77.9bn equity value and $108.4bn enterprise value at that stage. Paramount investor presentation filed with SEC.

19–20 JAN 2026

Netflix agreement is amended to all cash

The $27.75 per-share value is converted to all cash. WBD shareholders would also receive the value of the separated Discovery Global business, while the cash paid for the retained business remained subject to the agreed net-debt adjustment mechanics. WBD Form 8-K.

24 FEB 2026

Paramount’s revised $31 proposal could become superior

WBD says the revised offer could reasonably be expected to lead to a Company Superior Proposal. Terms included $31 cash, ticking consideration after 30 September, a $7bn regulatory termination fee, payment of Netflix’s $2.8bn termination fee and additional equity support if needed for the lenders’ solvency certificate. The Netflix agreement remained in force at this point. WBD filing.

26 FEB 2026

WBD formally determines Paramount’s proposal is superior

The board makes the contractual determination under the Netflix merger agreement and gives Netflix a four-business-day matching period. WBD announcement.

27 FEB 2026

Netflix waives its match right; Paramount agreement signed

WBD terminates the Netflix merger agreement, Paramount pays the $2.8bn Netflix termination fee on WBD’s behalf, and WBD, Paramount Skydance and Prince Sub sign the definitive merger agreement. Paramount also terminates its tender offer as the parties move to a negotiated merger. WBD Form 8-K.

12 JUN 2026

US DOJ closes its antitrust investigation

The DOJ says its review found the transaction was not likely to harm competition in SVOD, linear television or theatrical film development, production and distribution. DOJ statement.

6 AUG 2026

UK CMA clears the transaction

The CMA concludes there is no realistic prospect of a substantial lessening of competition in the UK and closes its inquiry on 17 August. CMA case page.

6 OCT 2026

Transaction closes

Prince Sub merges into WBD; WBD survives as a wholly owned subsidiary of Skydance Corporation. WBD shareholders receive $31.01666668 per share in cash, including ticking consideration. Closing Form 8-K.

03 / counterparties

The companies

Paramount Skydance

At the time of the bid, Paramount Skydance was the combined public company created by the 2025 Paramount–Skydance transaction. David Ellison led the company. Its assets included Paramount Pictures, CBS, Paramount+, Nickelodeon, sports and a global television network portfolio. It wanted a larger content library, studio footprint, streaming base and distribution reach.

Do not confuse Paramount Global (the pre-combination company), Skydance Media (the private production business), Paramount Skydance Corporation (the bidder) and Skydance Corporation (the renamed surviving parent after the WBD closing).

Warner Bros. Discovery

WBD combined WarnerMedia’s studio, HBO, news and sports assets with Discovery’s unscripted and international network portfolio. Its strategic challenge was not simply “bad assets”: premium studios and streaming coexisted with a structurally challenged linear-network portfolio and a capital structure that constrained flexibility.

The sale process offered shareholders a choice between a separated-company route and a sale of the entire group.

Netflix

Netflix wanted Warner Bros.’ film and television studios, HBO and HBO Max. Those assets could deepen its content pipeline, franchise portfolio and premium programming. Its structure avoided acquiring the entire cable-heavy Global Networks business, which would have brought different growth, capital allocation and regulatory considerations.

Prince Sub Inc.

Prince Sub was a wholly owned acquisition subsidiary of Paramount Skydance. It was the legal merger vehicle. At closing, Prince Sub merged with and into WBD, and WBD remained in existence as the wholly owned subsidiary of the buyer.

05 / competing proposals

The bidding war: Netflix versus Paramount

The headline prices were not directly comparable. Netflix’s proposal bought a defined set of assets after a separation and included a net-debt adjustment plus a separate Global Networks distribution. Paramount’s proposal bought all of WBD for cash and assumed the whole-company balance-sheet and operating exposure.

How Paramount’s proposal evolved

8 December 2025

$30 per share · all cash

Paramount commenced a tender offer for all WBD shares. The SEC-filed presentation described a $77.9bn equity value, $108.4bn enterprise value, $40.7bn new cash funding and a $54bn bridge loan. Those were proposal-stage figures, not final drawn financing.

24–27 February 2026

$31 per share · enhanced protections

Paramount added ticking consideration, a $7bn regulatory termination fee, payment of Netflix’s $2.8bn fee, additional equity support for the solvency certificate and an exclusion for Global Networks performance from the relevant material-adverse-effect definition. The final merger agreement carried $31 plus the contractual ticking amount.

Why the entire-company bid mattered: Netflix’s offer required WBD to separate and distribute Global Networks. Paramount offered to acquire the full corporate group. This was not simply “$31 beats $27.75”; the board had to compare different asset perimeters, debt allocation, retained shareholder interests, closing paths and contractual protections.

Primary references: Paramount’s February 2026 proxy solicitation / offer narrative; Netflix initial terms; WBD 24 February revised proposal notice.

06 / board decision

How Paramount won

The board did not immediately declare the first Paramount offer superior. On 24 February, it said the revised proposal could reasonably be expected to lead to a superior proposal and continued discussions. On 26 February, after consultation with independent financial and legal advisers, it determined that Paramount’s revised proposal constituted a Company Superior Proposal under the Netflix agreement.

What was a Company Superior Proposal?

A defined contractual test, not just a higher headline bid. The board had to assess whether the competing proposal was more favourable to WBD shareholders, taking account of financial terms, conditions, financing, regulatory risk and other relevant factors under the Netflix agreement.

What were matching rights?

Netflix had a specified period after receiving notice to propose revisions. It could seek to improve its own agreement so Paramount’s bid would no longer qualify as superior. Netflix waived the right to propose revisions, clearing the way for WBD to terminate.

On 27 February WBD terminated the Netflix agreement and Paramount paid the $2.8bn termination fee to Netflix on WBD’s behalf. WBD cancelled the special meeting scheduled for 20 March. The same-day Form 8-K records the sequence. Read the filing.

07 / documentary reconstruction

Behind the negotiations

The most useful public reconstruction is the SEC proxy material filed in connection with Paramount’s solicitation of WBD shareholder proxies. It describes the chronology of approaches, proposals and communications. It is a party-filed document: it is a primary source for what Paramount disclosed, but its arguments and characterisations should not automatically be treated as neutral conclusions.

What is documented

  • Paramount stated it had delivered six proposals over approximately 12 weeks.
  • It commenced a $30 all-cash tender offer on 8 December 2025.
  • The board process included discussions involving WBD management, board representatives and financial/legal advisers.
  • WBD’s February notices document the superior-proposal determination and Netflix matching period.
  • The executed agreement records the final consideration, financing commitments and termination protections.

What cannot be claimed

  • Private motivations beyond what participants publicly stated.
  • Undisclosed conversations or negotiations not in filings or credible reporting.
  • That an adviser gave a fairness opinion unless the relevant opinion and its scope are identified.
  • That all financing commitments were funded at signing.
  • That management’s synergy targets have already been achieved.

Advisers and process evidence

WBD’s financial advisers identified in the proxy-related materials include Allen & Company and J.P. Morgan. The public record also identifies legal advisers and transaction participants in the detailed proxy chronology. Paramount’s solicitation material is advocacy prepared by the bidder, not a neutral board report. Adviser attribution should therefore be taken from the party-specific proxy and merger filings, not inferred from a press article or a list of banks financing the bridge.

09 / commercial logic

Why acquire these businesses?

Studios and production

Paramount Pictures and Warner Bros. bring production operations, distribution capabilities, film slates and relationships with talent. Scale can improve the ability to finance, market and distribute a wider slate, but the two studios still compete for creative projects, release dates and audience attention.

Content libraries and franchises

Warner Bros. and HBO contribute a deep catalogue and recognisable franchises; Paramount adds its own library and brands. A library can be monetised through theatrical releases, streaming, licensing, television windows and international distribution. Owning a title is not the same as holding every right in every territory, medium or time period.

Streaming

Paramount+ and HBO Max bring subscriber relationships, technology, recommendation systems, billing infrastructure and content portfolios. A bundle or combined offering may reduce churn or improve customer acquisition economics, but execution depends on product quality, pricing, rights and technology integration.

Television, news and sports

CBS, CNN, TNT Sports and other networks add reach, live programming, advertising inventory and distribution relationships. These assets may support cross-promotion and broad audience reach, while also carrying exposure to declining cable bundles, expensive sports rights and editorial-independence scrutiny.

Independent analysis

The strategic thesis is about combining complementary content and distribution assets while gaining scale in a market where streaming economics are difficult. The risk is that the combined company also inherits overlapping costs, complex rights portfolios, a large linear-network exposure and a demanding debt and integration agenda. The asset list alone does not prove that the deal creates value.

10 / sector context

Why media consolidation became relevant

Traditional television relied on bundled cable subscriptions and affiliate fees, supported by advertising. Streaming changed the customer relationship: viewers can subscribe directly, cancel quickly and choose from multiple services. That creates a global market opportunity, but also raises customer-acquisition costs, churn, content spending and technology investment requirements.

Linear television pressure

As households move away from cable bundles, networks face pressure on affiliate fees and advertising reach. Live news and sports can remain valuable because they are time-sensitive, but rights costs and audience fragmentation matter.

Streaming economics

Scale can spread technology, marketing and content costs across more subscribers. Yet a larger subscriber base does not automatically mean higher profit: content amortisation, licensing, churn, customer support and pricing determine contribution economics.

Content monetisation

Owning IP can allow repeated exploitation across windows and formats. However, exclusivity decisions involve trade-offs: licensing creates near-term revenue, while keeping content exclusive may strengthen a proprietary service.

Competition

Netflix, Disney, Amazon and Apple compete through different combinations of subscription, advertising, commerce, devices, sports and broader ecosystems. A legacy media group cannot assume it can match the balance sheets or platform economics of technology companies.

Financing the acquisition

The final financing package combined substantial equity support with debt financing. The key lesson is to distinguish a commitment from funding actually drawn at closing.

Equity commitment

An investor’s contractual promise to subscribe for shares, subject to agreed conditions. It supports the buyer’s ability to fund the transaction.

Debt commitment / bridge

A lender’s promise to provide borrowing capacity under defined terms. Bridge financing is typically temporary funding intended to be refinanced or replaced by longer-term capital.

Funded equity

Equity capital actually contributed and issued at or around closing. It is different from an undrawn subscription commitment.

Drawn debt

Borrowings actually advanced and outstanding. Do not treat the face amount of a debt commitment as the debt balance.

Primary source: WBD merger agreement 8-K, 27 February 2026; WBD closing 8-K, 6 October 2026.

12 / approvals and scrutiny

Regulatory review

9 JUNE 2026

UK CMA opens merger inquiry

The CMA begins a formal inquiry following its initial information-gathering process. CMA case timeline.

12 JUNE 2026

US DOJ closes its investigation

The DOJ Antitrust Division says the transaction is not likely to harm competition in SVOD, linear television or theatrical film production and distribution. This is the DOJ’s stated conclusion following its investigation, not a universal guarantee that no stakeholder concerns existed. DOJ statement.

6 AUG 2026

CMA announces Phase 1 clearance

The CMA finds no realistic prospect of a substantial lessening of competition in the UK. It later publishes the full decision and closes the case on 17 August. CMA case page.

Do not mix up these legal processes: an antitrust investigation is the regulator’s evidence-gathering and analysis; merger clearance is the decision that the transaction may proceed under that regime; a court proceeding is litigation before a court; a settlement or undertaking is a negotiated resolution or commitment; and a contractual regulatory condition allocates the consequences between buyer and seller if approval is not obtained.

Media ownership and editorial independence are distinct from conventional product-market competition. The combined company owns major news brands, which makes governance, editorial safeguards and public trust relevant post-close questions. Any claim that a political actor directed editorial outcomes requires specific evidence; ownership alone does not establish that.

14 / post-close

After closing: what is known by 24 November 2026

Completed / documented

Corporate structure and listing

WBD became a wholly owned subsidiary of Skydance Corporation. The parent changed its name from Paramount Skydance Corporation to Skydance Corporation. The Class B common stock moved to the NYSE and ticker SKYD. Skydance 8-K.

Announced target

Cost transformation

Skydance has stated a target of at least $6bn of annualised run-rate synergies within three years. It described more than 20 workstreams. This is a target, not a realised saving. November 9 investor filing.

Announced plan

Streaming

Management has discussed a bundled HBO Max and Paramount+ offering in early 2027. The bundle is a forward-looking plan; it should not be described as launched as of this research cut-off.

Announced commitment

Content output

The closing announcement referred to a minimum 30 theatrical films per year and 180+ television shows and series as output commitments. Treat these as announced commitments, not as verified post-close output achieved.

What investors should monitor over the next three years

  • Net debt, cash interest, refinancing timetable and progress toward stated leverage objectives.
  • Actual synergy savings net of restructuring costs and reinvestment.
  • Subscriber churn, average revenue per user, streaming contribution margin and bundle adoption.
  • Studio release cadence, box-office performance, production budgets and licensing revenue.
  • Linear network cash generation and the pace of structural decline.
  • Retention of creative leadership and talent, and the quality of the content pipeline.
  • Editorial independence safeguards and governance around CBS News and CNN.
15 / independent view

Was this a good deal?

Buyer’s perspective

Strategic case: compelling breadth across studios, streaming, sports, news and distribution. Trade-off: Paramount bought more than premium content; it also took on legacy networks, integration complexity and substantial financing demands. The deal’s value depends on disciplined execution and cash generation.

Seller’s perspective

Strategic case: a whole-company cash offer avoided a pre-close separation and delivered a higher fixed cash amount than the Netflix retained-business payment. Trade-off: WBD shareholders surrendered future participation in a separately listed Global Networks company.

Shareholder’s perspective

Outcome: eligible WBD shareholders received $31.01666668 per share in cash at closing. Risk before closing: completion depended on conditions, regulatory review and financing performance. The ticking amount compensated for delay after the agreed date.

Industry’s perspective

Potential benefit: a larger competitor with a broader library and distribution footprint. Potential cost: concentration, overlapping studios and reduced buyer choice for creative suppliers, alongside pressure on employment and production economics.

Conclusion: The acquisition is strategically understandable, but it is not possible to call it a value-creating success merely because it closed. The buyer won the auction by improving price and certainty protections, but the post-close business must now demonstrate that scale can translate into sustainable free cash flow without damaging the creative assets that justified the purchase.
16 / learning desk

M&A concepts explained through this transaction

Strategic buyer

A company buying another business to add capabilities, assets or market reach. Paramount sought studios, franchises, streaming and distribution scale.

Unsolicited proposal

An approach not invited or agreed as part of an existing negotiated deal. Paramount’s approach competed with WBD’s Netflix agreement.

Tender offer

A public offer made directly to shareholders to buy their shares on stated terms. Paramount commenced a $30 cash tender offer on 8 December 2025.

Negotiated merger agreement

A contract negotiated with the target and approved by its board, setting the terms and conditions for a merger. The parties signed on 27 February 2026.

Superior proposal

A competing offer that satisfies the definition in the existing agreement after the board applies the required process. Paramount’s revised offer was determined to qualify on 26 February.

Matching rights

A contractual opportunity for the existing bidder to revise its deal after receiving notice of a superior proposal. Netflix had four business days and waived the right to propose revisions.

Termination fee / break fee

A fee payable when a deal ends in specified circumstances. WBD’s Netflix agreement required $2.8bn in the relevant superior-proposal termination scenario.

Reverse termination fee

A fee the buyer pays if the deal fails in specified circumstances, often regulatory failure. Paramount agreed to $7bn for specified regulatory termination events.

Equity commitment letter

A contractual commitment from an investor to provide equity subject to agreed terms. The Ellison Trust and RedBird made subscription commitments.

Fairness opinion

A financial adviser’s opinion on whether consideration is financially fair from a specified perspective, based on stated assumptions and limitations. It is not a guarantee that a deal is optimal or that it will close.

Conditions precedent

Conditions that must be met or waived before the parties must complete. Regulatory and shareholder approvals are common examples.

Signing and closing

Signing creates the binding agreement; closing is when the transaction is legally completed after conditions are satisfied or waived. Here the dates were 27 February and 6 October 2026.

Synergies

Incremental benefits expected from combining businesses, such as eliminating duplicate overhead or improving distribution. Skydance’s $6bn figure is a target, not a realised outcome.

Integration risk

The risk that combining systems, teams, cultures and operations disrupts the businesses or destroys value. It is significant when combining two studios, streaming services and large network portfolios.
